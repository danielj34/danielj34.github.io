01RTL / VERIFICATION / FPGA

Independent project · 2026

A processor,
one cycle at a time.

Source & testbench ↗

A synthesizable, single-cycle RV32I CPU in SystemVerilog. The interesting part was making every register write, memory strobe, and branch target agree with the instruction being executed.

What I built

A 32 × 32-bit register file, ALU, decoder, immediate generator, branch and jump logic, and separate instruction/data-memory interfaces. A Python encoder turns test programs into instruction words.

How I checked it

Self-checking cocotb/Verilator regressions examine execution cycle by cycle, including signed comparisons, byte-store lanes, jump links, misaligned accesses, and precise traps.

Make it easier
to reach.

TEAM PROJECT
3 PROTOTYPE ITERATIONS

A wheelchair-mounted grabber developed around limited hand dexterity and reach. The challenge extended beyond closing a claw to mounting, balance, control effort, and picking things up from the floor.

PROJECT ARTIFACTS

My contribution

My work included concept development, ESP32 and PCB prototyping, and electromechanical integration. My early hook concept explored retrieving everyday items with minimal finger input. The team combined ideas and refined the device through three prototype iterations.

The team’s final device

A telescoping rod, geared motor-driven claws, a flexible wheelchair attachment, and an accessible handle. The final report records roughly 1 kg total mass and 6 hours of printing for the final PLA components.

↳

The decision I’d talk about in an interview

The final controller was a switch.

The initial electronics used an ESP32, TB6612FNG motor driver, and three buttons. Reliability, packaging, and control usability problems led the team to a simpler 2P2T switch circuit. It reduced the circuit footprint and made opening and closing the gripper more direct.

The mechanical design changed too. The gripper was angled upward by 45° to improve floor pickup, and wooden dowels replaced weaker printed axles.

TESTING LIMITS

The report records gear-to-motor slipping and PLA failure under load. Comfort and usability were tested with student proxies and simulated mounting, so client validation and further durability work remained open.
